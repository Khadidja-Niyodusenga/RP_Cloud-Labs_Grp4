# Requirements Elicitation Techniques for RP-Cloud Labs

## Overview

Requirements elicitation is the practice of collecting, discovering, and defining system requirements from stakeholders, end-users, and domain experts. Below are two primary elicitation techniques, their detailed process, and their application to specific stakeholder groups within the RP-Cloud Labs project.

---

## 1. Interviews

### Definition & Explanation
A direct, interactive conversation (structured, semi-structured, or unstructured) between the software development team and a stakeholder or user to explore expectations, system goals, business rules, and technical needs.

### Process
1. **Preparation:** Define clear objectives and draft targeted open-ended questions.
2. **Execution:** Conduct the interview, ask probing questions, listen actively, and take detailed notes.
3. **Follow-Up:** Summarize the conversation and share key points with the stakeholder for validation.

### Key Pros & Cons
* **Pros:** Allows deep exploration of complex processes, immediate clarification of vague answers, and builds strong working relationships.
* **Cons:** Time-consuming to schedule and conduct; relies heavily on stakeholder availability and clear communication.

### Matched Stakeholder Group
**RP-Cloud Labs System Administrators & Lecturers / Lab Instructors**

### Why this technique is suitable for this group in RP-Cloud Labs:
1. **Deep Technical & Functional Requirements:** System admins and lecturers deal with complex operational rules (e.g., virtual machine provisioning, resource limits, lab scheduling, and student grading). Interviews allow developers to ask in-depth follow-up questions to understand these technical workflows clearly.
2. **Uncovering Specific Pain Points:** Lecturers face practical challenges while running lab sessions. Direct dialogue helps expose hidden bottlenecks in lab delivery and student monitoring that structured forms might miss.
3. **High Impact, Small Group Size:** Since the number of admins and lead instructors for RP-Cloud Labs is small relative to students, conducting one-on-one sessions yields maximum qualitative value efficiently.

---

## 2. Surveys and Questionnaires

### Definition & Explanation
A structured list of predefined questions (both quantitative multiple-choice and qualitative open-ended) distributed electronically to collect feedback from a broad target audience.

### Process
1. **Design:** Create clear, concise, and unbiased questions using tools such as Google Forms or Microsoft Forms.
2. **Distribution:** Share the survey link through official communication channels to target groups.
3. **Analysis:** Aggregate numerical results into charts and analyze open responses for recurring trends.

### Key Pros & Cons
* **Pros:** Highly scalable, cost-effective, reaches a large audience quickly, and provides quantifiable data.
* **Cons:** Risk of low response rates; inability to ask immediate real-time follow-up questions if answers are ambiguous.

### Matched Stakeholder Group
**RP Students (End-Users / Learners)**

### Why this technique is suitable for this group in RP-Cloud Labs:
1. **Scalability Across Large Student Population:** Students form the largest user cohort of RP-Cloud Labs. A digital survey allows the development team to gather data from hundreds of students across multiple campuses simultaneously.
2. **Measuring General Usability & Performance:** Standardized rating scales make it easy to analyze core user experience metrics such as platform accessibility, lab loading speeds, navigation ease, and feature satisfaction.
3. **Anonymity Encourages Candid Feedback:** Students are more likely to share honest criticisms regarding system bugs, slow performance, or usability issues when completing an anonymous survey.

---

## Conclusion

Combining Interviews for administrators/lecturers and Surveys for the student body ensures that RP-Cloud Labs captures deep operational functionality alongside widespread usability and user satisfaction.
